Introduction
 The Texas FAIR Plan Association (TFPA) was established by Insurance Code Chapter 2211 for the
 purpose of delivering residential property insurance to qualified citizens of Texas in areas determined
 by the Commissioner of Insurance to be underserved areas.

 House Bill 998, passed during the 88th Texas Legislative session, requires the Texas FAIR Plan
 Association to offer property owners’ association insurance, meaning property and liability insurance
 covering common areas and facilities of a homeowners’ association, or common elements of a
 condominium owners’ associations in specific areas designated by the Insurance Commissioner within
 ten miles of Texas Windstorm Insurance Association’s (TWIA) coverage area.

General Rules
The TFPA Commercial Property Owners Association Program provides property and liability coverage,
using the forms and endorsements specified in this Manual. This Manual contains the rules and
classifications governing the writing of the property owner’s insurance provided by TFPA. The rules,
form and endorsements of the TFPA, for each coverage, shall govern in all cases specifically provided
for in this Manual.

This Manual contains the necessary underwriting references to guide the agent in writing the available
insurance coverage for TFPA eligible applicants. Where applicable, the TFPA Plan of Operation will
govern when any discrepancies may exist between this Manual and the Plan of Operation.
An agent and/or agency may not require as a condition for writing a TFPA policy the sale or previous
purchase of another policy (i.e., auto, life, health, etc.) with the agent and/or agency.

Program Eligibility and Underwriting Guidelines
Program Eligibility Requirements
 Property Owners are eligible for Commercial multiperil coverage if they are not currently insured by
 an authorized insurer and:

     1. At least two insurance companies, not in the same holding company as defined in
        Insurance Code section 823.002, licensed to write and actually writing commercial
        property insurance in Texas have declined to provide commercial property insurance (the
        names of the two insurance companies shall be identified), and the applicant has not

          received a valid offer of comparable commercial property insurance from an insurance
          company licensed in Texas, not including any surplus lines insurers; and

      2. There are no outstanding taxes, assessments, penalties, or charges with respect to the
         property to be insured, except those covered under a properly filed deferral affidavit in
         compliance with §33.06 of the Property Tax Code;
      3. The applicant has not received written notice from an authorized public entity stating that
         the property is in violation of any building, housing, air pollution, sanitation, health, fire or
         safety code, ordinance, or rule;
      4. Not Vacant or unoccupied; and
      5. Not under repair or renovation.

Agents must establish eligibility prior to quoting and placing a policy with the Texas FAIR Plan
Association and shall maintain and submit at the request of the Association, written documentation of
(1) – (3). Failure to provide proper evidence of declination subjects the policy to cancellation.

The property to be insured is subject to all other applicable underwriting criteria.
